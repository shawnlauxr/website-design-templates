CLIENT-FRIENDLY HOSTING GUIDE

How much does it cost to keep a website online?

Think of it this way: a domain is your website’s address. Hosting is where your website lives. A database is an extra service that remembers information, like customer accounts and bookings.

Proof of conceptS$0

For these offline demos. No domain, hosting or database is required.

Simple websiteS$1–40/mo

About Us, portfolio, company details and photos. Usually no database fee.

Website that saves informationS$30–85/mo

For logins, profiles, bookings or a dashboard. A database may add cost.

Example choices

Cheapest simple company site: use free static hosting from an eligible provider and pay for a custom domain, often just a few dollars per month when its yearly renewal is averaged out.

Website on Vercel Pro: Vercel Pro begins at US$20/month (around S$27–30), plus any separately purchased domain and usage beyond included credits.

Website with database: add a managed database such as Supabase Pro from US$25/month (around S$34–36), if a paid database is necessary. You normally do not need to rent and maintain a separate physical SQL server.

These are examples, not required purchases. Not every backend feature needs a paid database; some services have free usage allowances. All costs exclude applicable taxes, excess usage and currency-conversion fees.

Who pays and who manages it?

The client owns the accounts and pays the providers directly. Whether the website developer provides ongoing maintenance is a separate decision. Maintenance fees and one-time website development fees are not included in the prices here.

Which one should you choose?

If your website is just about your business, services and portfolio, start with a simple website. There is no reason to pay for a database that you don't need.

If visitors need to log in, reserve something with custom booking rules or save personal information, then discuss whether a database or hosted third-party service is necessary.
